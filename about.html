About

Built by a woman who's done the recognizing herself.

100 Women Society grew out of one honoree list — and a belief that recognition should come with real accountability behind it.

The founder

Erica Thomas Adeyemi

Erica is a former Georgia State Representative for District 39, one of the youngest members of any state's legislative body, and one of the first orphans to hold political office in the country. During her time in office she was Vice Chair of the House Democratic Caucus and Communications Chair for the Georgia Black Caucus.

She created Foster Care Day in the State of Georgia, founded Birthday Wish to celebrate the birthdays of foster children statewide, and helped bring the Black Music and Entertainment Walk of Fame to Mercedes-Benz Stadium in partnership with the City of Atlanta.

Her recognitions include a Presidential Lifetime Achievement Award from President Barack H. Obama, the Ambassador for Peace Award from the Global Peace Foundation, 100 Black Men of America's Image Award, Apex Society's "Power 30 Under 30," Atlanta's Who's Who "Most Influential" Award, and TV One's Triumph Award through the National Action Network.

Why 100 Society

Recognition is the beginning, not the end.

Erica has spent years building the stage — producing large-scale events honoring icons from T.D. Jakes to Run DMC, and more recently curating focused, mission-driven rooms of women. The 100 Women of Excellence Recognition Ceremony is the newest of those rooms. 100 Society exists so the honor doesn't end when the ceremony does — it becomes a year of accountability, exposure, and access among the women who share it.

Vision

An ongoing society, not a one-day event

Every woman recognized becomes part of a standing community — with weekly calls, event invitations, member highlights, and cross-promotion built to outlast any single ceremony.
